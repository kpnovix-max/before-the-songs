A kingdom after the songs

The novel

Across the water, they remembered differently.

The songs had been written.

They had given the brave their names, the wicked their faces, and the dead their proper places.

But across the water, there were people who remembered differently.

There are things the sea does not return.

A sandal washed from a child’s foot. A wooden toy that slips between the planks of a boat. The sound of a familiar voice calling from another room. A house with its lamps still burning, though nobody who lived there will ever see it again.

And there are things the sea carries to the other shore.

Children who have forgotten their names. Women clutching bundles of clothing that belong to people who did not come. Men who can no longer remember whether they were brave. A little girl who has been told she is safe, and who cannot understand why everyone keeps saying it.

The boats have arrived.

The fires are far away now. Salt dries upon the children’s faces. Wet clothes hang between trees whose names nobody knows. Someone is boiling water. Someone is counting the living. Someone has begun to sing, very softly, because there is a child who will not sleep.

The girl listens.

She has been listening for something else.

There was a time when she knew exactly where she belonged. She knew the sounds of her home, the smell of its corridors after rain, the particular footsteps that meant someone she loved had returned. She knew which questions would make him laugh, and which ones would make him put aside his work and listen.

Now she knows very little.

The years will give her other things. A place to live. People to love. Ordinary mornings. The small consolations by which a life becomes possible again.

And stories.

Stories of the city that burned. Stories of those who escaped. Stories of those who stayed. Stories of things that happened, and things that might have happened, and things that everyone eventually agrees must have happened.

Some will be told in anger.

Some in gratitude.

Some simply because the children ask.

The girl will remember more than most. She will also forget things. There will be days when she is certain of something she cannot prove, and days when a familiar memory changes without her noticing.

For now, none of that matters.

She is fourteen years old.

She has survived the end of one world.

She does not yet know how many others will be made from it.
